# Episode 4: Cooler and Motherboard into the Case

**Runtime:** ~10 min · **Goal:** mount the cooler correctly and get the board into the case without shorting it.

## Parts and tools checklist
- [ ] Cooler (AIO or tower) with mounting kit for your socket
- [ ] Thermal paste (if not pre-applied)
- [ ] Isopropyl alcohol 90%+ and lint-free cloth
- [ ] Case with standoffs; I/O shield if the board doesn't have one built in
- [ ] Magnetic-tip screwdriver, small parts tray

## Script

**0:00 Cold open.** [CU] Paste dot on the CPU. "Pea-sized. Not a pancake."

**0:30 Why the cooler matters.** Introduce the Math Box: heat has to flow from the chip to the air, and paste fills the microscopic air gaps.

**2:00 Paste and mount.** Pea or X pattern. Peel the cooler's plastic film (the classic mistake). Tighten screws in an X pattern, a few turns each, until snug.

**4:30 Prep the case.** Count the standoffs and match them to the board's holes. "A standoff with no hole above it can short the board."

**6:00 I/O shield, then board in.** Shield first (if separate), tilt the board in, line up the ports, start all screws by hand, then tighten.

**8:00 AIO radiator placement.** Top mount, exhaust. Pump should not be the highest point in the loop, so air bubbles collect in the radiator.

**9:30 Teaser.** "Next time: the graphics card, the power supply, and making the cables look good."

## Math Box: why paste and a good cooler matter

Temperature rise follows a simple law, like Ohm's law for heat:

  **Delta T = P x R_th**

where P is heat in watts and R_th is thermal resistance in degrees C per watt.

Example: CPU at 142 W, cooler plus paste total R_th = 0.30 C/W, room at 25 C:

  Delta T = 142 x 0.30 = 42.6 C
  CPU temp = 25 + 42.6 = **~68 C**

Forget the paste and the air gap might push R_th to 0.60 C/W:

  142 x 0.60 = 85.2, so 25 + 85.2 = **~110 C**, past the chip's limit; it throttles.

"Same CPU, same cooler. A gram of paste is the difference."
